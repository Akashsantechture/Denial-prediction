SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM WE ARE SOLVING
================================================================================

  HEADLINE:
  Claim denials are a preventable revenue problem

  BODY POINTS:

  The cost of denials
  ───────────────────
  - Healthcare providers across the UAE lose significant revenue annually
    to claim denials that could have been prevented at submission
  - Each denied claim requires manual review, rework, and resubmission —
    multiplying administrative cost per claim
  - The longer a denial goes unaddressed, the lower the probability of
    successful recovery

  Why denials happen
  ──────────────────
  - ICD and CPT code mismatches that violate payer clinical logic
  - Claim amounts outside expected ranges for the procedure and setting
  - Short billing lags indicating insufficient documentation time
  - Encounter type and facility mismatches with the billed services
  - Payer-specific patterns that are not visible to billing staff

  The gap today
  ─────────────
  - Billing teams submit claims without real-time feedback on denial risk
  - Post-denial review is reactive, not preventive
  - There is no tool that explains WHICH specific element of a claim is
    the primary driver of denial risk for that individual claim

  The opportunity
  ───────────────
  - Intervene at the moment of submission, before the claim reaches the payer
  - Identify the exact driver and give the billing team a targeted action
  - Reduce denial rate, reduce rework cost, protect revenue

SLIDE 4 — DATA FLOW DIAGRAM
================================================================================

  [RESERVED FOR DIAGRAM]

  Suggested title: "ClaimAI — End-to-End Data Flow"

  Six-step flow:

  Step 1 — User submits claim
    Form fields or JSON → browser collects ClaimRequest object
    (19 fields + N activity lines)

  Step 2 — Prediction
    POST /predict → predictionapi.py
    XGBoost scores each activity → SHAP TreeExplainer decomposes each score
    Returns: claim score, per-activity probabilities, SHAP drivers

  Step 3 — Frontend renders results
    Overview: Claim Score Hero + activity table + gauge
    SHAP tab: Waterfall + scatter + dependence charts (per activity)
    ICD / Interactions: Population reference heatmaps

  Step 4 — AI session created
    Browser POSTs claim intelligence to /analyst/session (port 8060)
    Gemini session initialised, claim evidence stored in PostgreSQL
    Badge: "✦ Gemini AI"

  Step 5 — User chats
    POST /analyst/chat → Gemini reasoning against claim evidence
    If Gemini fails → local rule engine (transparent fallback)

  Step 6 — Session cleanup
    DELETE /analyst/session on new submission
    PostgreSQL cascade-deletes all session data

SLIDE 5 — THE CLAIMAI SOLUTION
================================================================================

  HEADLINE:
  Real-time denial prediction with explainable AI

  BODY:

  What it does
  ────────────
  ClaimAI scores every claim before submission against a trained XGBoost
  model. In under 200 milliseconds it tells the billing team:

    - The Claim Denial Score (composite probability, 0–100%)
    - Which activity on the claim is at highest risk and why
    - The exact model features driving the score, ranked by impact
    - A specific action to take to reduce the risk before submitting

  How it explains itself
  ──────────────────────
  Every prediction is accompanied by SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
  values — a mathematically rigorous method that decomposes the model's
  output into individual feature contributions for that specific claim.

  This is not a generic explanation. It is the actual reason the model
  scored that specific claim the way it did.

  What makes it different
  ───────────────────────
  - Trained on UAE healthcare claims data
  - Per-activity SHAP analysis, not just a claim-level black box
  - Multi-activity claim support built in
  - Conversational AI analyst powered by Gemini 2.5 Flash
  - Fully containerised, API-first, integrates into any RCM workflow
  - Works entirely in the browser — no installation, no client software

SLIDE 6 — MODEL PERFORMANCE & EVIDENCE
================================================================================

  HEADLINE:
  A model built for healthcare, validated on real claims

  SECTION A — Model Specifications

  Algorithm:       XGBoost (gradient-boosted decision trees)
  Device:          CUDA (GPU-accelerated training)
  Features:        19 per activity line
  Output:          Denial probability 0–100% per activity

  SECTION B — Performance Metrics

  ROC-AUC:         0.8466
  Overall Accuracy: 76%
  Denied Recall:   75%  — catches 3 in 4 actual denials before submission
  Denied Precision: 67%  — 2 in 3 flagged claims are genuinely at risk

  SECTION C — Top 5 Predictive Features (by information gain)

  1. Activity Gross Amount       17.53%
  2. CPT Category                16.98%
  3. Activity code         14.19%
  4. Net Claim Amount            11.44%
  5. Billing Lag Days             5.56%
  ─────────────────────────────────────
     Top 5 combined:             65.70%

  SECTION D — Claim Scoring Formula

  Claim Denial Score = 0.7 × (highest activity risk)
                     + 0.3 × (average activity risk)

  This composite weights the most dangerous activity line heavily, because
  a single high-risk procedure is typically sufficient to trigger payer denial
  of the entire claim.

  Risk Bands:
  LOW       <  40%   Standard processing
  MEDIUM    40–70%   Review before submission
  HIGH      ≥  70%   Manual review required

SLIDE 7 — THE AI ANALYST: GEMINI-POWERED CLAIM INTELLIGENCE
================================================================================

  HEADLINE:
  Ask questions. Get answers grounded in the model's evidence.

  BODY:

  What it is
  ──────────
  The AI Analyst is an embedded conversational interface that lets billing
  staff ask plain-English questions about any claim that has just been scored.
  It is powered by Google Gemini 2.5 Flash via Vertex AI.

  What makes it trustworthy
  ─────────────────────────
  Gemini does not hallucinate answers about this claim. Before the conversation
  starts, the full claim intelligence — all 19 feature values, the model's
  denial probability, and the complete SHAP evidence — is sent to Gemini as
  structured context. Every response Gemini gives is grounded in that evidence.
  The system prompt explicitly prohibits responses that are not supported by
  the provided claim data.

  Example conversations
  ─────────────────────
  "Why is activity 99213 flagged as high risk?"
  → Gemini explains the top SHAP drivers specific to that activity line

  "What would reduce the claim score most?"
  → Targeted remediation steps based on the highest-impact SHAP driver

  "How does the billing lag affect this claim?"
  → Specific analysis of the billing lag SHAP value and its direction

  "Compare all activities on this claim"
  → Side-by-side analysis of every activity's denial probability and drivers

  Resilience
  ──────────
  If Gemini is unavailable, the platform falls back silently to a local
  rule-based engine. The badge in the chat header changes from "✦ Gemini AI"
  to "⚡ Rule-based". Every other feature of the platform continues to work
  without interruption.

SLIDE 8 — EXPLAINABILITY: WHAT THE CHARTS SHOW
================================================================================

  HEADLINE:
  Every prediction is transparent, auditable, and actionable

  SHAP Waterfall Chart
  ────────────────────
  Shows how each of the 19 model features shifted the prediction away from
  the population baseline (−0.32 log-odds). Red bars push toward denial.
  Green bars push toward approval. The waterfall always closes at the model's
  exact output — it is not an approximation.

  Feature Value vs SHAP Value
  ────────────────────────────
  A dot plot where each dot is one feature for the selected activity.
  The horizontal position shows whether the feature is pushing toward denial
  (right) or approval (left). The colour shows the strength of that push
  from blue (weak) to red (strong).

  SHAP Dependence — Activity Gross
  ─────────────────────────────────
  Shows how the billed gross amount relates to its SHAP contribution for
  this specific activity (the diamond marker). Background dots are
  illustrative trend points. If the diamond is above zero, the amount is
  a denial driver. If below zero, it is reducing denial likelihood.

  ICD × CPT Interaction Heatmap
  ──────────────────────────────
  Population-level view of which diagnosis-procedure pairings have the
  highest combined denial signal in the training data. Helps billing teams
  identify structurally problematic claim patterns before they become denials.

  Activity Selector
  ─────────────────
  For multi-activity claims, every chart can be instantly switched to show
  the analysis for any individual activity line via a dropdown. The
  highest-risk activity is pre-selected by default.

SLIDE 9 — DEPLOYMENT, SECURITY & OPERATIONS
================================================================================

  HEADLINE:
  Production-ready, containerised, and operationally sound

  SECTION A — Deployment

  Both services are fully containerised with Docker:

  Prediction API image
    python:3.11-slim + XGBoost + SHAP + FastAPI
    Stateless — scales horizontally without any configuration change
    Cold start: ~3 seconds. Subsequent requests: <200ms

  AI Analyst image
    python:3.11-slim + Google Cloud SDK + PostgreSQL driver
    Google Cloud credentials mounted as a secret (not baked into the image)
    Conversation sessions are ephemeral — auto-deleted on new submission

  Orchestration: Docker Compose for development.
  Production: Any container orchestration platform (Kubernetes, ECS, Azure
  Container Apps). Services are independent and can be scaled separately.

  SECTION B — Data & Privacy

  - No patient PHI is persisted by the prediction service. The API is
    stateless — input flows in, prediction flows out, nothing is stored.
  - The AI Analyst stores session data in PostgreSQL for the duration of
    the conversation only. Sessions are explicitly deleted (CASCADE) when
    the user submits a new claim.
  - The request log table in PostgreSQL stores operational metadata only
    (latency, status, endpoint) — no clinical field values.

  SECTION C — MLOps

  - Models are versioned and tracked in MLflow
  - Data drift and concept drift are monitored continuously
  - Automated retraining trigger fires when drift metrics exceed thresholds
  - New model versions are validated against champion before promotion
  - All prediction inputs and outputs are logged for audit and retraining
