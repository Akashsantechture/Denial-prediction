────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q1.  What problem does ClaimAI solve?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Healthcare providers in the UAE submit thousands of insurance claims every
month. A significant portion of those claims are denied by payers, requiring
costly manual review, rework, and resubmission. The root cause is almost
always the same: the claim was submitted without understanding why payers
deny it in the first place.

ClaimAI solves this at the point of submission. Before a claim goes out,
the platform scores it against a trained machine learning model, identifies
every feature driving the denial risk, and tells the clinical billing team
exactly what to fix — in plain language, within seconds.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q2.  What is the core technology powering the predictions?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The prediction engine is XGBoost — a gradient-boosted decision tree
classifier — trained on UAE healthcare claims data. The model takes 19
input features per activity line (diagnosis code, CPT/activity code, claim
amount, billing lag, clinician type, facility type, payer classification,
ICD-CPT domain match, and others) and outputs a denial probability between
0 and 100%.

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) TreeExplainer runs on every prediction
to decompose the model output into per-feature contributions. This is what
allows the platform to say precisely which feature is pushing a specific
claim toward denial — not a generic explanation, but the actual mathematical
reason computed by the model for that individual claim.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q3.  How is the claim-level denial score calculated?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A healthcare claim can contain multiple activity lines (procedures billed
together). The model scores each activity independently because each has
its own CPT code, gross amount, and procedure-specific features.

The claim-level score is then computed as a weighted composite:

    Claim Score = 0.7 × highest_activity_risk + 0.3 × average_activity_risk

This formula intentionally weights the worst activity more heavily because
a single high-risk activity line is usually sufficient for a payer to deny
the entire claim. The resulting score is what appears on the Overview tab
as the Claim Denial Score.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q4.  What are SHAP values and why do they matter clinically?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SHAP values are a game-theory-based method that fairly distributes a model's
prediction among its input features. For ClaimAI, each SHAP value represents
the contribution of one feature to the log-odds of denial for a specific
activity, measured in log-odds units.

Clinically, they answer the question: "Of all the things about this claim,
which one is most responsible for the high denial score?" A billing team
member does not need to understand log-odds — they see: "Activity Gross
Amount is pushing this claim toward denial with a score of +0.94." That
is actionable. They can check the billed amount, verify it is correct, or
flag it for review before submission.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q5.  How accurate is the model?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The XGBoost model achieves the following on the held-out test set:

    ROC-AUC:           0.8466
    Overall Accuracy:  76%
    Denied Recall:     75%  (catches 3 in 4 actual denials)
    Denied Precision:  67%  (of flagged claims, 2 in 3 are genuinely denied)

The top five features by information gain are:
  1. activity_gross (17.53%)
  2. cpt_category (16.98%)
  3. activity_code (14.19%)
  4. claim_net (11.44%)
  5. billing_lag_days (5.56%)

These five features alone account for approximately 66% of the model's
predictive power.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q6.  What does the platform actually look like to a user?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The user opens a web browser, navigates to the platform URL, and sees a
clean claims entry form. They enter the claim details — diagnosis code,
procedure codes, amounts, billing lag, patient demographics, and provider
information — either by filling individual fields or by pasting a JSON
payload directly.

After clicking Evaluate, they see within seconds:

  - A Claim Denial Score (the composite risk percentage)
  - A colour-coded verdict: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW
  - A per-activity breakdown table showing each procedure's denial risk
  - SHAP waterfall, scatter, and dependence charts explaining the drivers
  - ICD Drill-Down and Interactions tabs showing population-level context
  - An AI chat assistant they can ask questions in plain English

Everything runs in the browser. No installation required.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q7.  What is the AI Analyst and how does it work?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The AI Analyst is a conversational panel embedded in the platform. It can
answer natural language questions about the claim that was just scored —
why it was flagged, what each SHAP driver means, which activity is at most
risk, and what specific steps the billing team should take.

Technically it operates on two levels:

  Primary (Gemini AI): When the AI Analyst microservice is running, the
  chat is powered by Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash model via Vertex AI. The
  full claim intelligence — all 19 feature values, the model prediction,
  and the SHAP evidence — is sent to Gemini as structured context. Gemini
  then generates grounded, evidence-based responses about that specific claim.

  Fallback (Rule-based): If the Gemini service is unavailable for any reason,
  the platform transparently falls back to a local rule-based engine. The
  user experience is identical — responses are still grounded in the real
  SHAP values and claim data, just generated deterministically rather than
  by the LLM.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q8.  What data does the AI Analyst have access to?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The AI Analyst has access to exactly the information that was submitted and
scored in the current session:

  - The raw claim fields (ICD code, CPT code, amounts, demographics, etc.)
  - The model's denial probability and risk classification
  - The SHAP values for every activity on the claim
  - The per-activity verdict and recommendation from the prediction engine
  - The conversation history within the current session

It has no access to other patients' claims, payer policy documents, external
databases, or the internet. All responses are grounded in the claim that is
currently on screen. The conversation history is stored in PostgreSQL during
the session and automatically deleted when the user submits a new claim.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q9.  What are the system's main components and how do they connect?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The platform has three runtime services:

  1. Prediction API (port 8000) — FastAPI + XGBoost + SHAP. Accepts the
     claim payload, runs the 19-feature inference pipeline, computes SHAP
     values via TreeExplainer, and returns the full prediction response.
     Also serves the frontend as static files.

  2. AI Analyst Microservice (port 8060) — FastAPI + Gemini (Vertex AI) +
     PostgreSQL. Manages conversational sessions. Receives the claim
     intelligence from the browser, stores it in PostgreSQL, and on each
     chat turn builds a structured prompt and calls Gemini.

  3. Browser SPA — Vanilla JavaScript + Plotly.js. The frontend is a pure
     HTML/CSS/JS single-page application with no framework dependency. It
     calls both backend services directly via fetch() and is the
     orchestrator — neither backend service talks to the other.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q10. How is the platform deployed?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Both services are containerised with Docker. A docker-compose.yml file
defines the full stack: the Prediction API container, the AI Analyst
container, and a PostgreSQL database container. The entire system can be
brought up with a single command:

    docker compose up --build

For cloud deployment, each service has its own Dockerfile and can be
deployed independently to any container orchestration platform (Kubernetes,
AWS ECS, Azure Container Apps, etc.). The Prediction API image uses
python:3.11-slim with XGBoost and SHAP. The Analyst image includes the
Google Cloud credentials and Vertex AI SDK.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q11. What are SHAP interactions and why is there a dedicated tab for them?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Some features in the model do not operate in isolation — their combined
effect on denial likelihood is stronger than the sum of their individual
contributions. The most significant of these is the ICD × CPT interaction:
the pairing of a diagnosis code category with a procedure category.

The Interactions tab shows a population-level heatmap of all ICD group ×
CPT group combinations from the training data, colour-coded by their
combined SHAP contribution and by their observed denial rate. This allows
a billing team to see at a glance which diagnosis-procedure pairings are
historically problematic — not just for the current claim, but across the
entire claims population. For example, Pathology & Laboratory procedures
paired with Neurological diagnoses show among the highest combined denial
signals in the training data.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q12. What does the ICD Drill-Down tab show?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The ICD Drill-Down tab provides a population-level reference view for every
ICD-10 code group in the training dataset. For each code it shows the
observed denial rate, the mean SHAP contribution across all training claims
with that code, and the average model-predicted probability.

When a claim is submitted, the tab automatically highlights the row matching
the submitted ICD code group in amber — so the billing team can instantly
see how the current claim's diagnosis sits relative to the historical denial
rates for that entire code category. A separate heatmap shows denial rates
broken down by ICD group and encounter type (Outpatient, Inpatient, Emergency).


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q13. How does the platform handle the case where the AI Analyst is offline?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The platform is designed to be fully functional without the AI Analyst
microservice. The fallback is seamless:

  - When a claim is submitted, the browser attempts to create a session
    on the AI Analyst (port 8060) in the background, after the greeting
    is already shown to the user.

  - If the attempt fails (service down, network error, timeout), the chat
    badge switches to "⚡ Rule-based" and the local rule engine handles
    all responses.

  - If the LLM call fails for an individual message (Gemini quota, timeout),
    only that single message falls back. The next message tries Gemini again.

  - The prediction results, all charts, and all four tabs work entirely
    independently of the AI Analyst service.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q14. What is the billing lag feature and why does it matter?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Billing lag is the number of days between the service date and the date
the claim was submitted to the payer. It has a 5.56% information gain in
the model — the fifth most important feature.

Counterintuitively, the model assigns higher denial risk to short billing
lags (0–3 days). The training data shows that claims submitted very quickly
after the service date tend to be denied more often, likely because rushed
submissions are associated with incomplete or inaccurate documentation.
Longer lags, while potentially raising other flags, are associated with
more thorough documentation and lower denial rates in the model's learned
patterns.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q15. Can the platform handle multi-activity claims?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Yes. The claim form includes a dynamic activity table where billing staff
can add as many activity lines as the claim contains. Each activity has its
own CPT code, quantity, gross amount, and CPT category.

The model scores each activity independently, producing individual denial
probabilities and SHAP breakdowns for each line. The Overview tab shows all
activities in a single table with per-row denial probabilities, verdicts,
and top SHAP driver pills. The SHAP Analysis tab has an activity selector
dropdown that lets the user drill into the detailed SHAP charts for any
specific activity line.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q16. How is this different from a simple rules engine?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A traditional rules engine checks explicit if-then conditions that a human
programmed — "deny if CPT is X and diagnosis is Y." It cannot detect subtle
statistical patterns, interactions between features, or population-level
trends that were never explicitly programmed.

ClaimAI's XGBoost model learned its decision logic from the actual historical
claims data — including patterns that are not intuitive or obvious to a
human. It captures non-linear relationships (activity gross above a certain
threshold behaves differently depending on the CPT category), interaction
effects (ICD × CPT domain mismatch amplifies other denial signals), and
demographic patterns (nationality interacts with encounter type in ways that
a rule engine would not capture).

The SHAP layer then makes that learned logic transparent and actionable,
which a black-box rules engine cannot do.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q17. What is the MLOps strategy for keeping the model current?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The platform is built with an MLOps lifecycle in mind:

  Data Pipeline: Raw claims data is validated, feature-engineered, and
  versioned before entering the training pipeline.

  Training: XGBoost models are trained and tracked in MLflow, which records
  hyperparameters, metrics, and model artifacts. Every model version is
  registered in the MLflow Model Registry before deployment.

  Monitoring: Prediction logs are stored and monitored for data drift (input
  feature distribution shifts), concept drift (model performance degradation),
  and prediction volume anomalies. When drift metrics exceed thresholds, a
  retraining trigger fires automatically.

  Retraining: New training runs are kicked off against the latest validated
  data. The new model version is evaluated against the current champion before
  any promotion to production. Docker images are rebuilt and redeployed.

Q18. How long does a prediction take?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A standard single-activity claim prediction — including SHAP computation —
completes in approximately 100–200ms on typical server hardware. Multi-activity
claims with 5–10 activity lines take proportionally longer (roughly 300–500ms
for 10 activities) because SHAP is computed for each activity row independently.

The first prediction after server startup takes slightly longer (~2–3 seconds)
because XGBoost's first inference call involves JIT compilation. Subsequent
calls are fully cached in memory.

The frontend applies a 20-second timeout to prediction requests to handle
worst-case cold-start scenarios. The AI Analyst chat responses from Gemini
have a separate 30-second timeout.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q19. What does the platform NOT do, and what are the current limitations?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Being transparent about current limitations:

  - The ICD Drill-Down and Interactions tabs show population-level data from
    the training dataset, not live database queries. As payer denial patterns
    change over time, this reference data will need periodic updates.

  - The SHAP Dependence chart background dots are synthetic illustrations, not
    real historical claims. Only the diamond marker (this claim) is real data.

  - The model was trained on a specific UAE claims dataset. Generalisation to
    significantly different payer networks or clinical settings has not been
    validated.

  - The AI Analyst cannot currently access payer-specific policy documents,
    prior authorisation requirements, or external coding guidelines. This is
    a planned RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) upgrade.

  - There is no batch processing endpoint yet. Claims must be submitted one
    at a time through the web interface or the REST API.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Q20. What is the roadmap and what comes next?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Three near-term capabilities are planned:

  Phase 2 — RAG-enhanced AI Analyst: Embed a knowledge base of payer denial
  codes, UAE HAAD/DHA guidelines, prior authorisation requirements, and
  clinical coding references into a vector store. The AI Analyst would then
  ground its answers in both the SHAP evidence AND the relevant policy
  context — giving billing teams citation-backed explanations tied to actual
  payer rules.

  Phase 3 — Batch scoring API: A /predict/batch endpoint accepting arrays of
  ClaimRequest objects, enabling integration into existing RCM (Revenue Cycle
  Management) systems. Claims would be scored nightly or in real time via
  webhook, with results written to a reporting database.

  Phase 4 — Appeal letter generation: Using the SHAP evidence and the
  AI Analyst's reasoning capabilities to auto-draft denial appeal letters
  pre-populated with the specific clinical justification for each denied
  activity line, referenced to payer policy codes.
